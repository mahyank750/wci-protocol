## Open, Interdisciplinary Call for Contributors

The World Commonwealth Index (WCI) is an open research project. We invite
scholars, practitioners, and interested contributors worldwide to help examine,
test, and improve its concepts, indicators, methods, data, and governance.

Contributions are welcome from, but are not limited to:

- Mathematics, statistics, econometrics, and measurement theory
- Economics, monetary economics, finance, banking, and accounting
- Sociology, social policy, family studies, and anthropology
- Psychology, public health, medicine, and wellbeing research
- Philosophy, ethics, political theory, and human rights
- Environmental science, climate research, and sustainability
- Political science, public administration, law, and governance
- Data science, software engineering, and open-source development
- Education, demography, and other relevant fields

### Open questions—including weights

The proposed indicators, aggregation methods, penalty factors, and weights
are provisional and open to review. This includes the proposed emphasis on
family and social stability, including any 50% weighting shown in earlier
materials. That figure is a proposal to be examined—not a settled scientific
or moral fact.

We especially welcome well-supported alternatives concerning:

- Whether family wellbeing should be a separate dimension, how it should be
  defined, and how it can be measured without privileging one family form,
  culture, religion, or country;
- Whether the proposed weight is justified, and what alternative weights or
  weighting methods are preferable;
- How to combine dimensions, account for uncertainty, avoid double-counting,
  and test sensitivity to assumptions;
- How to design penalties for severe harms without unfairly blaming vulnerable
  people or entire populations for actions beyond their control;
- Which indicators and datasets are valid, comparable, transparent, and
  ethically appropriate across countries and cultures.

No discipline or contributor has an automatic monopoly on these questions.
Strong proposals should explain their reasoning, identify assumptions, cite
reliable sources where possible, and state how the proposal could be tested.

### How to contribute

- **Discuss an idea:** Open a GitHub Issue describing the question or proposal.
- **Suggest a change:** Submit a Pull Request with the proposed text, method,
  formula, or code, together with its rationale and sources.
- **Review evidence:** Point out limitations, missing evidence, data-quality
  issues, or possible unintended consequences.

Constructive criticism and better alternatives are welcome. Proposals will be
considered transparently on their reasoning, evidence, feasibility, ethical
implications, and reproducibility—not on the contributor’s status or
institutional affiliation.

The project's founding purpose is to explore a more human-, social-, and
ecological-wellbeing-oriented framework for economic measurement. That purpose
is open to discussion and refinement; it does not make any particular formula,
weight, indicator, or penalty immune from review. Changes and their rationales
should be documented publicly so that the project’s development remains
traceable.
